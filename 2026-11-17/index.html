Today’s lead · Agents & security

Google says attackers built an AI system that reads security patches, works out the flaw and exploits it, with no human in the loop

Google’s threat team found a framework called exploit_agent on an exposed attacker server. It is the first time Google has seen vulnerability research and exploitation fully automated in a real operation.

On Monday 16 November, Google’s Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) published its quarterly AI Threat Tracker. Its main finding is a tool its operator called exploit_agent, found on a staging server the attacker had left exposed. It is a multi-agent system connected to several frontier models and to models running on the attacker’s own hardware.

The method is called patch diffing. When a vendor ships a security fix, comparing the code before and after shows where the hole was. Skilled people have always done this by hand. According to Google, exploit_agent does it continuously for popular web software. It works out which flaw was fixed, writes an exploit and runs it against sites that have not updated yet. Reported targets include French media and education websites. The configuration comments were in Russian, but GTIG did not name a country.

Confirmed vs. not: this is Google’s finding about a real attacker’s tooling, not a lab demo. But Google named no specific vulnerability, gave no victim count and did not measure how fast it goes from patch to exploit.

Why it matters: the FBI’s ARTEX warning (covered Sunday) was about agents exploiting old weaknesses fast. exploit_agent targets the gap between a fix being published and being installed. Automate that, and the time from “patch released” to “patch exploited” shrinks. Our reading, not a Google checklist: for internet-facing web software, updating quickly matters more than it did.

Safety · announced 16 November

OpenAI releases a free classifier for child-abuse images, built with NCMEC, and adds warnings and 24-hour suspensions in ChatGPT

The classifier detects child sexual abuse material (CSAM), including AI-generated images. It was built with the US National Center for Missing and Exploited Children and is shared via the Tech Coalition and the nonprofit ROOST. OpenAI says it sends 48% fewer images to human review than its existing tool while finding about as many abusive ones; that is its own estimate, not an audit. ChatGPT will also warn users who seek such material and suspend them for 24 hours. Fewer false alarms matters because human reviewers are the bottleneck.

Research · released 11 November

DeepMind’s AlphaGenome 2 predicts how a DNA change affects one specific type of cell, not just a whole tissue

Most disease-linked genetic variants don’t break a gene; they change when it is switched on, often in only one kind of cell. The first AlphaGenome predicted across broad tissues. Version 2 takes a specific cell type or state as input. The neat part: researchers can describe a new cell state with data from a few dozen cells, without retraining. DeepMind’s example tells vaccine-activated immune cells apart from resting ones. It reports finding about 30% of known causal variants on an immune benchmark (outlets cite different baselines, 16% or 23%). Available via API for non-commercial research. Six days old, but not covered here before.
