Today’s lead · Policy & impact

Google tells schools: switch off AI for students and Google Search goes with it

Under new Workspace for Education controls, schools can no longer keep plain Search while blocking Google’s AI. They get both or neither.

Google announced on Tuesday new controls for student accounts in Workspace for Education, the email, docs and Classroom suite many US schools run on. Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Search will be off by default for students, and administrators switch each one on. The catch is that Search can’t be on while AI is off. A school that keeps AI disabled loses Search too, and students see a “Search unavailable” message.

Google’s education VP, Chris Phillips, says Search and AI are too closely intertwined to separate. That is a product claim, and it treats AI Overviews as part of Search itself, not an optional layer. Reports point to early 2027, though one outlet says Google has given no firm date.

Google has “prioritized its business interests over the education of our children.” — Randi Weingarten, American Federation of Teachers, as quoted in coverage of the announcement

Why it matters: the classroom debate used to be whether to allow AI. Now a school that wants to keep AI out has to give up the world’s main search engine to do it. The change only covers school-managed accounts, but it shows where Google is heading: AI is becoming the core of Search, not a feature you can turn off.

The Quick Scan

Platforms & agents

Apple opens Siri AI to all developers in the iOS 27.3 betas, but apps can’t ship it until spring

Through the 27.3 betas, any developer can now plug an app into Siri AI using App Intents, Apple’s framework for describing what an app can do. Siri can then check a delivery or book a table, and chain several actions together. A new Siri Evaluations framework lets developers script test requests and score the answers, which is a notable step toward testing agents properly. App Store submissions reportedly open in spring, and Apple’s usage policies are still unpublished.

MacRumors, 3 Nov · PPC Land

Open models

Tencent’s 770-billion-parameter Hy4 adds vision and computer use

Hy4 is the first model in Tencent’s Hy line that can read images and operate software, both on the command line and through graphical interfaces. The training targeted tools such as Blender and CAD. It is available via Tencent and OpenRouter at about $0.83 per million input tokens. Tencent says weights will follow “shortly,” but unlike the Apache-licensed August preview, they are not yet downloadable.

TechNode, 4 Nov

Policy

China says it is accelerating a “full-lifecycle” AI law

Wang Ruomeng, an official at the National Development and Reform Commission, said on Tuesday that Beijing is speeding up legislation covering AI from data and compute through to deployment. Reports say developers would have to disclose safety risks and report incidents, with a sandbox for controlled testing. No draft text or timetable has been published, so treat this as intent, not new rules.

Build · company claim

OpenAI says GPT-6 Astra designed a talking Codex gadget in under a week

OpenAI says two hardware engineers, as a side project, had GPT-6 Astra design a push-to-talk device for its Codex coding agent: the enclosure, the circuit board (component choice and routing), the firmware and the UI, with the aim of avoiding direct human edits. A contract manufacturer built prototypes within a month. That step took several revision rounds, because the model missed tolerances and assembly details.

Signal Check

Today’s biggest AI story wasn’t about what models can do. It was a default setting that schools can’t fully undo.

Deserves attention: Google’s school bundling. Making Search depend on AI is a product decision, not a technical necessity. Expect other platforms to argue the same way, that their AI can no longer be separated from the core product.

Overstated: “AI designed hardware in a week.” The design phase was fast. Getting to physical units still took a month and several manufacturing revisions, and OpenAI has published no comparison with a human team, hours worked or quality bar.

Uncertain: when Google’s change takes effect, whether Tencent’s Hy4 weights actually appear, and whether Moonshot meets its own promise to publish the Kimi K3.1 Pro weights by today, 4 November. That was not confirmed when this brief was written.
