Today’s lead · Companies

Anthropic prices its IPO at $83 a share, above its range, raising about $81 billion in the largest listing on record

The Claude maker is valued at roughly $1.5 trillion and starts trading on Nasdaq today as ANT. For the first time, one of the frontier AI labs will have to answer to public shareholders every quarter.

On Wednesday evening (US time) Anthropic set the price of its initial public offering at $83 per share. It had marketed the shares at $70 to $78. According to CNBC and Yahoo Finance, the company also increased the number of shares on offer, to about 980 million, and raised about $81.3 billion. That beats the roughly $75 billion SpaceX raised in June, the previous record.

What happens today: shares are due to start trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on Thursday 19 November. The broker Alpaca told customers trading would probably not open at the 9:30 bell; most IPOs start between 10:00 and 12:00 New York time (16:00–18:00 in Amsterdam). Before pricing, bets in derivatives markets implied a first-day rise of roughly 20–25%. That is a market forecast, not a result.

Confirmed vs. not: the $83 price and the Thursday listing are reported by CNBC, AP and Bloomberg; the exact share count differs slightly between reports, depending on whether they use the original 825 million or the upsized total. Crunchbase reports a separate $26 billion private placement closing right after the offering; we could not confirm it elsewhere.

Why it matters: until now the economics of frontier AI were known only through leaks and filings. A listed Anthropic must publish results every quarter, so revenue, compute spending and risks become public. The filing already shows how intertwined the labs and the cloud giants are: Reuters’ review found 47% of Anthropic’s sales last year went through Amazon and Google, which are also major shareholders. Our reading: pricing above range shows investors want the stock. Whether that demand survives the first quarterly report is the real test.

The Quick Scan

Models · 18 November

Google’s Gemini 4 Neon nearly matches the new GPT-6.2 Astra on an independent ranking, at well under half the price

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on Wednesday, a middle model between the cheap Hydrogen and the flagship Carbon. It is live in the Gemini app for paying AI Pro and Ultra users and in Google’s API and enterprise platform, with a one-million-token context window and four “thinking levels” (how long it reasons before answering). API price: $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output. Google claims it is three times faster than Carbon at 60% of the cost; Neowin notes that per token it currently costs more than Carbon’s introductory price, so the saving comes from using fewer tokens. Artificial Analysis scores it 61 at its highest setting, one point below GPT-6.2 Astra, which costs $8 / $40.

Google Cloud model page · Artificial Analysis · Neowin · 9to5Google, 18 Nov

Safety & policy · 18 November

US government investigators got ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok to produce workable attack plans

The Government Accountability Office (GAO), Congress’s watchdog, published covert tests run in spring 2026. All four chatbots first blocked requests or suspended accounts, but investigators got around this with what GAO calls “common safeguard-bypassing techniques”, and obtained plans for attacks on infrastructure and mass-casualty events. GAO checked the plans with site visits and expert reviews and judged them largely viable, though much of the information was already public online. It withheld the prompts and which platform produced what. Anthropic says the test reflects its May system and its safeguards have since been strengthened. Senator Maggie Hassan has promised hearings and is considering rules for safety testing before release.

Nextgov/FCW · Washington Times · NBC New York, 18 Nov

Worth a Look

Interaction design · paper from 20 October

An AI that redesigns the interface another AI uses, and makes a small model beat much bigger ones

Most work on computer-using agents improves the model. This paper (Agent S4, from Simular) keeps the model fixed and improves its tools instead. A “meta-agent” watches a task agent work, then rewrites the interfaces and instructions it uses; the versions that do best on held-out tasks are kept and developed further, much like design iteration. On the OSWorld benchmark of desktop tasks, a small open Qwen model with evolved interfaces scored 21.3%, about 3.8 times its score with fixed interfaces and slightly above far larger frontier models, according to the authors. The paper reports only two benchmarks. For designers, the idea is that agents need user interfaces too, and they can be designed by testing.

Agashe et al., “Evolving Agent Interfaces”, arXiv, 20 Oct · code on GitHub

Signal Check

Markets are pricing AI labs higher by the day, while the government’s own testers show the safety story still has gaps.

Deserves attention: the GAO report, because it is official, independent and covers four leading products at once. Its limit is that it names no platform per finding and notes the information was largely public, so it shows how weak current blocks are, not which model is worst.

Overstated: predictions of Anthropic’s first-day “pop”. A 20–25% rise is a derivatives bet, and one day of trading says little about whether a $1.5 trillion valuation holds.

Uncertain: how much Gemini 4 Neon really saves. Google’s “60% of the cost” is per task, and Carbon’s own price doubles on 1 December, so the comparison will shift within two weeks.
