Today’s lead · Agents & security

The FBI warns that criminals and state hackers are using ARTEX, an open-source AI attack tool, sometimes reaching admin access in under three hours

An FBI flash alert says a multi-agent hacking framework, built for authorised security testing, is now used in real attacks across at least ten sectors. It mostly exploits old weaknesses, just very fast.

The FBI published alert FLASH-20261113-001 on Friday 13 November, after our previous edition’s research had closed. It describes ARTEX, an open-source penetration-testing framework (software that security testers use to attack systems with permission). You give it a goal in plain language, and a team of AI agents turns that goal into commands: finding targets, breaking in, gaining higher privileges and copying data out. ARTEX does not include its own model. It plugs into outside models, including Claude, GPT, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok and Kimi.

What the FBI says: this is confirmed misuse, not a lab demonstration. Targets include finance, government, defence, semiconductors, energy, healthcare, manufacturing, telecoms and law firms. Reported techniques are familiar ones: attacking public-facing apps, abusing trusted logins and powerful cloud permissions, and stealing the keys that control a Windows domain. Attackers mostly plug in Chinese models, especially DeepSeek Flash, which the FBI links to cost and weak guardrails. The developer took the GitHub repository down on 8 October, but forks, archived copies and a paid “ARTEX PRO” version still circulate.

The detail that matters: the FBI writes that ARTEX “generates high volumes of forensically noisy traffic that may trigger alerts, but attack speed provides limited time for coordinated defender response.” In other words, security tools notice it, but by the time a person reacts, the attacker may be done.

Mitigation: reported summaries say the alert cites no zero-days (unknown, unpatched flaws). The raw material is exposed admin panels, hard-coded passwords and keys that are never rotated. The first recommendation reported is to stop putting management interfaces on the internet without an architecture review. We could not read the full FBI list, so check the alert itself before acting on it.

Why it matters: “AI-powered hacking” has mostly meant research demos or single campaigns. This is a US law-enforcement warning that a free, agent-based tool has gone mainstream among attackers. Old security debt now gets found and used in hours, not weeks.

The Quick Scan

Agents · launched 13 November

OpenAI’s ChatGPT Wallet lets its agents check out for you, with a Face ID or fingerprint check on every purchase

Wallet works inside conversations with “dots”, OpenAI’s agents, not in ordinary ChatGPT chats. At launch it is limited to US Pro subscribers aged 18+ with an American Express, Mastercard or Visa credit card. The agent pays with a payment token (a stand-in number) and never sees the real card number. You approve each purchase yourself. Reports say OpenAI has hinted at spending caps and category limits later. OpenAI itself warns that some shops may block these purchases and that fraud systems may flag them. Coverage notes that refunds and agent mistakes are not fully explained. Context: a day earlier, United Airlines banned agent bookings (covered yesterday). In Visa’s own benchmark from 5 November, 22 models failed 42.8% of shopping tasks, and in many failed runs they still bought something.

Agents & tools · released 12–13 November

Cloudflare open-sources Kitesurf, a lightweight browser engine built for AI agents rather than people

Kitesurf loads pages, runs JavaScript and lets agents click and type, using less memory and CPU than a full Chromium browser. It is built from open-source Rust parts (Blitz for layout, Stylo for CSS) and runs each page in its own isolated Cloudflare Worker. It speaks the Chrome DevTools Protocol, so existing Puppeteer scripts connect without rewrites. It is now on GitHub under Apache 2.0. Two catches: it needs a paid Workers plan, and Unite.AI notes it ships without authentication and exposes a public URL, so a careless self-hosted setup is open to anyone. Cloudflare calls it experimental and still under heavy development. For web builders, it is a sign that agents are becoming their own kind of browser, one your site will increasingly serve.

Worth a Look

Robotics · open release, about 11 November

Runway, known for AI video, has released an open robot-control model that learned how the world moves by watching video

Praxis-1 starts from a video model that predicts the next few moments of a scene. A small “action expert” reads that model’s internal picture of what is about to happen and turns it into robot movements. The idea is that a model which can imagine how objects move can also learn how to move them. Runway says Praxis-1 has 27 billion parameters and scores 46.2% on NVIDIA’s RoboLab-120 test, against 28.0% for the well-known π0.5. Those are Runway’s own numbers. The weights are on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0, and developers can adapt it to their own robot and cameras with a small add-on (LoRA) instead of retraining the whole model. It is a good example of creative-AI technology moving into the physical world. The release date comes from a third-party write-up; Runway first previewed the model in September.

Signal Check

The agent story right now is less about smarter models than about who lets agents act, and how fast an agent acting against you can move.

Deserves attention: the ARTEX alert. It is confirmed misuse, documented by the FBI, and it uses the same agent techniques the industry is selling for productivity. The defence it calls for is unexciting: fewer exposed admin panels, rotated keys, faster response.

Overstated: “your AI agent can now shop for you.” Wallet still needs your fingerprint for every purchase, shops may block it, and Visa’s own test shows agents getting the purchase wrong about four times in ten.

Uncertain: the FBI’s full mitigation list, which we could not read directly, and how markets react to Nvidia on Tuesday and Anthropic’s pricing on Wednesday.
