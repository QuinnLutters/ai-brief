Today’s lead · Research

An Anthropic mathematician says he and Claude proved the Riemann hypothesis, releasing only computer-checked code and no readable paper

The proof checker accepts the code, which is more than past claims had. But no human has read the argument, and the mathematical community has not accepted the result.

On 20 November Levent Alpöge, a mathematician who works at Anthropic, posted a four-page note on his personal website: “I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.” The Riemann hypothesis, first stated in 1859, says where the zeros of a particular function (the zeta function) lie. It governs how primes are spread out, is one of the Clay Institute’s million-dollar Millennium Problems, and has defeated every earlier claimed proof. Alpöge says the method also covers the stronger Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH).

What was released: not a paper but code in Lean, a “proof assistant” language in which a computer checks every logical step. The files are split into 14 downloads. A footnote says the informal, AI-generated written material ran past 250,000 pages, which is why only the formal version was published.

What has been checked: MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland says he independently compiled the code and that it relies only on Lean’s “three standard axioms”, meaning no extra assumptions were slipped in; Scientific American’s follow-up headline calls the proof “almost certainly true”. Mathematician Elliot Glazer said the formal statement in the release “is in fact GRH”. Not yet settled, according to the reports: whether every formal definition truly matches the hypothesis as mathematicians mean it, and a final run of a standard checking tool. Lean guarantees the steps follow, not that the theorem says what its authors claim.

What is missing: any explanation a human can follow, and any statement of which Claude model was used or what it contributed. Anthropic has not issued an official announcement; the claim is Alpöge’s own. Terence Tao called it an “enormous” Lean-only proof and has raised the broader worry that such proofs can be verified without anyone understanding them.

Why it matters: if it holds, it is the first problem of this rank to fall with AI doing much of the work. It follows Anthropic’s August result on the share of zeros in the right place (41.6% to about 67%), which Anthropic said would not lead to a full proof, and an October OpenAI-linked proof of a weaker “quasi-Riemann” version. Our reading: the machine check makes this claim far more credible than usual; it also creates a new situation in which mathematics may have to accept a result before anyone can explain it.

Agents & security · confirmed 19 November

The European Commission says an “AI-generated cyber campaign” knocked out staff payroll and HR systems for more than 10 days

A Commission spokesperson, Thomas Regnier, said the first signs were detected on 22 October and described the incident as an “AI-generated cyber campaign”. The disruption hit personnel systems for staff, including payroll, leave requests, medical reimbursements and translation services. The Commission says it has found no evidence of a broad breach of staff data and knows of no ransom demand; the investigation is open. It did not name the tool or the attackers. Some outlets link the attack to ARTEX, the AI hacking framework the FBI warned about last week (our 15 November edition), but the Commission has not said so, so treat that link as unconfirmed.

Worth a Look

Agents & security · Google Chrome, 19 November

Google built a sealed-off fake internet, written and run by AI agents, to attack its own browsing agent

Chrome’s “auto browse” agent clicks and types on websites for you, which makes every web page a possible source of hidden instructions (prompt injection). Google’s Chrome Security team describes “Mirror Dimension”: thousands of simulated sites, including shops, social feeds and bank dashboards, in a closed sandbox. Chrome sends the agent’s traffic to these local copies while the agent still sees familiar web addresses, so engineers can plant attacks without touching real sites. According to reports, the sites themselves are built by a team of agents: research agents define what a site needs, builder agents make the pages, critic agents check them.

A striking idea for anyone who designs interfaces: a whole web built for a machine user, to see where it gets fooled. Google has not published evidence that agents tested this way are safer on the real web, and a simulation cannot reproduce every quirk of live sites.

Unite.AI · GovInfoSecurity · PiunikaWeb, 19–20 Nov

Signal Check

A checked proof and an explained proof are no longer the same thing, and the Riemann claim is where that difference gets tested.

Deserves attention: the Lean result. Independent compilation by a respected mathematician is real evidence, far beyond the usual self-published claim.

Overstated: “AI solved the Riemann hypothesis”. The statement-matching check is not finished, no paper exists, and Claude’s contribution has not been described. Until those arrive, it is a strong claim, not an accepted theorem.

Uncertain: what the SEC is actually looking at in Nvidia’s books, and whether the Commission attack is the first of many. Neither has a confirmed scope yet.
